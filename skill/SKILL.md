## Client Reply Playbook (researched 2026-10-17: Kraya WA-B2B study + HubSpot objections + reply-handling skill; full research notes: /home/ubuntu/business-backup/research-whatsapp-b2b-sales.md)
**Speed:** reply within 1h — prospects are 7x more likely to qualify vs 24h delay. When a client messages, they are active + comparing + ready. 
**One question per message.** Never stack questions. Wait for the answer before the next step.
**Qualify conversationally (BANT):** need (what gear for?), timeline (when needed?), budget (qty?), authority (who decides?).
**Proposals in parts, not walls:** (1) restate what they said (2) solution (3) timeline+price (4) ask "works for you?" — each as separate short messages with acknowledgment between.
**Honesty rule (user's correction):** NEVER invent programs/discounts that don't exist. If unsure what client means, ASK them to clarify — a question is better than a fabricated answer.
**Trust signals:** real photos (never AI), specific specs, named clients/countries, 'quality speaks for itself - like the products you saw'. 
**Objection = information request:** acknowledge, answer once briefly, ask ONE clarifying question back. Not a reflex fight.
**Ambiguous replies (like Alvaro's 'content for you guys'):** do NOT assume + do NOT build castles. Two-move response: (1) honest: 'not sure I understood fully' OR reflect their words back as a question, (2) keep the existing offer standing. Let them explain their idea, THEN respond to the real meaning.
**Closing micro-steps:** always end with the smallest possible next action (send logo → mockup). Never ask for big commitments.
**System lines in chats:** a "Your security code with <name> changed" line is the CLIENT's own reinstall/phone change — not a security event on our side; ignore it in chat analysis.
**When they ask for better price:** never drop price first. Offer value: higher qty = better rate, or add free logo/belt to the sample.
**Silence after full info:** wait 24-48h, then breakup-lite message. Honor it — no more contact after a breakup message.
**Follow-up drafting standard (user-set):** follow-ups must be business-concrete — specific products, specific prices, and a next step that serves THEIR business (exact quote, side-by-side comparison, real photos, team-size question). NEVER the emotional/pleading register: no "give me a chance", no "no pressure", no "last message from me for now" — the user rejected that entire voice outright. A breakup message is still a short business recap (offer + price anchor + same-day quote promise + good wishes), never an emotional goodbye.
**Follow-up spacing:** never follow up a conversation whose last outbound from us is under ~24h old — it reads as spam to a lead who just received our question; skip them from the round unless the user explicitly orders an immediate send.
